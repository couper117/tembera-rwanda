S

Strengths

What is good about your project now.

  • You can now kick out a stolen loginNew Before, if someone stole your login, changing your password did nothing — they stayed in for 30 days. Now changing your password signs out every other device instantly, and there's a "sign out everywhere" button for a lost phone.
  • Password guessing is blocked After 5 wrong tries on an account, further attempts are refused for 15 minutes — on both the normal login and the admin one.
  • Login cookies cannot be forged or editedNew Now actually tested, unlike last time: changing the user number in a cookie, reusing an expired one, or backdating one to extend its life are all refused.
  • 94 automatic tests Covering search, distances, rankings, the password blocking and the login cookie. They run in about one second.
  • Your project notes are accurate Your HANDOFF file explains not just what you built but what you tried and rejected, and the traps that already cost you time. It's now up to date again.
  • Your work is safe and backed upNew Everything is saved in git and uploaded to GitHub on its own branch, so your main version is untouched until you're ready.
  • You handle personal data better than most A real privacy policy, users can download everything you hold, and they can delete their account.
  • You used a good, modern setup Next.js, TypeScript and PostgreSQL — what real companies use.
  • Everything is stored in the database You can add or change a place from the admin page without touching code.
  • The admin area is properly locked Every admin page and button checks "are you an admin?" first.
  • The site does not lie to users No invented ratings, no fake listings in empty categories.
  • It is a real, full product Search, maps, saved places, reviews, bookings and an admin system.

W

Weaknesses

What is left. You chose to leave these for later — that's a reasonable call.

  • Your place information is still not verifiedBiggest Nothing in either round of fixes touched this. About 200 listings have unconfirmed phone numbers, opening hours and locations. This is now clearly your biggest weakness — it decides whether the site is genuinely useful to anyone.
  • Your site cannot send email So there's no "forgot my password" — a user who forgets is stuck until you run a command for them. Also no way to confirm an email is real, and no booking confirmation. One decision fixes all three.
  • There's still no way to actually publish the site The checking stops at "it builds". No hosting is set up, and going live has never been practised.
  • Booking still doesn't take payment Properly validated now, but no payment, no availability check, no confirmation.
  • Admins still can't upload a photo Photos have to be web links from six approved sites.
  • Three security warnings remain — by your decisionDeferred They're inside Next.js itself and need a major upgrade. You chose to wait until after launch, which is sensible: they're hard to exploit here.
  • The password blocking resets when you restart It's held in memory. Fine for one server, weaker if you ever run several.
  • Every page still loads all 495 places Fine today. Only a problem if you reach thousands.
  • Your project folder is still in OneDrive It keeps locking files and breaking commands.

T

Threats

Dangers from outside. You can't stop these, but you can prepare.

  • Your old Google key is still liveUrgent The only urgent item left, and the only one costing real money. Removing it from your files did not cancel it — it's still readable in your project's history. Cancel it in the Google Cloud console.
  • Google Maps is free and already on every phone You win by having better Rwandan information than Google has — not by being another map.
  • You depend on Google for your map If they raise prices or change the rules, you follow. Your map also uses an old feature they'll eventually switch off.
  • The privacy policy points to a mailbox nobody readsReduced The law is mostly handled now — people can see and delete their data. But privacy@tembera.rw is a placeholder. A privacy policy nobody can reply to fails the first time someone complains.
  • Wrong information can cause real harm If someone drives across the country on a wrong address from your site, that's your reputation.
  • Information goes stale Businesses close and numbers change. Some of your photo links are already dead — that's the warning sign.
  • Everything still depends on you You're the only person who knows how this works. Less severe now that the work is backed up on GitHub, but still true.
  • Growing is what will break it The "loads all 495 places" problem only hurts once you're successful.

Part two

PESTEL: the world your project sits in

The SWOT above is mostly about your project. PESTEL is about everything around it — the six outside forces that will shape Tembera whether you think about them or not. Nothing here is something you control. All of it is something you can plan for.

For each of the six, the same three questions: what we think is going on, how Tembera fits it, how it doesn't — and then a straight answer to can it fit or not. No sitting on the fence.

P

Political

The government, and the rules it makes.

What we think The government wants the same thing you want: more tourists. It also owns the good, checked information about places. And it makes the rules for tourism businesses.

How it fits

  • The government spends money bringing tourists to Rwanda. You are helping with the same job. That makes people more willing to help you.
  • Rwanda likes local tech. A Rwandan-made app is something offices are happy to support.
  • It is easy for tourists to enter Rwanda. More tourists means more people who need your site.
  • The office with the best place information already wants tourism to grow.

How it doesn't fit

  • If there is trouble near the border, other countries tell their people not to visit. Bookings drop fast, and you can do nothing about it.
  • The same office you want to work with also makes your rules. So you are never fully your own boss.
  • Rwanda is a small market. What the government decides matters a lot to what you can do.
Can it fit?

Yes. This is the best of the six.

But you fit as someone helping the country's plan, not as someone doing their own thing on the side. Work with them. And don't plan as if every year will be a calm one.

E

Economic

Money — where it comes from, and how people pay.

What we think Rwanda has chosen to bring in fewer tourists who each pay a lot, instead of many who pay little. Seeing the gorillas costs about $1,500 on purpose. That one choice decides how you can make money.

How it fits

  • Your booking prices already match real prices. You got that right.
  • People who come to Kigali for big meetings have free evenings, money, and no idea where to go. That is exactly what your site is for, and almost nobody helps them.
  • Charging businesses to be listed, or taking a small cut of bookings, would work. Your database is already set up for both.

How it doesn't fit

  • You cannot ask Rwandans to pay a monthly fee. Most people cannot afford one.
  • Adverts won't work either. Adverts need huge numbers of visitors, and this market will never have them.
  • You pay for hosting and Google Maps in dollars, but you would earn in francs. And the Google bill grows as you get more popular.
  • If you only take card payments, most Rwandans cannot pay you. People here use mobile money.
Can it fit?

Yes — but only one way of making money will work.

Charge businesses, or take a small cut of bookings. Do not try to charge normal users, and do not plan on adverts. Choose now, because the two roads build very different apps.

S

Social

People — who uses it, and what they expect.

What we think You don't have one group of users. You have three: tourists from abroad, Rwandans here, and Rwandans living in other countries. They want different things, and they don't all read the same language.

How it fits

  • The name makes sense here straight away. "Tembera" means to travel.
  • It's built for phones, which is how people go online here.
  • Rwandans living abroad come home, spend like tourists, and are easy to reach online.
  • The government is telling Rwandans to travel their own country. That is building an audience for you.

How it doesn't fit

  • Your site is only in English. Fine for tourists. It leaves out most Rwandans.
  • Writing reviews is not a big habit here yet, so your review feature may sit empty for a while.
  • Genocide memorials are on your site. You cannot show them like restaurants. Stars and a "book now" button would upset people badly.
  • Your name may clash with a government campaign name. Nobody has checked.
Can it fit?

It fits tourists and Rwandans abroad. It does not yet fit ordinary Rwandans.

Kinyarwanda is the door. Until you add it, treat the local market as closed, not just slow. And decide how memorial pages should look before you launch, not after someone complains.

T

Technological

Phones, internet, and what your users actually have.

What we think Your users are on cheap Android phones. Their data is expensive. And the signal is weakest in the places tourists actually go.

How it fits

  • It's already built for phones.
  • You only have 495 places. That's small enough to save onto a phone and keep there.
  • The way it's built can support working without internet, without starting again.

How it doesn't fit

  • Right now it needs internet. So it stops working at the exact moment someone needs it — standing outside a park, deciding where to eat.
  • Nobody has opened it on a cheap phone. Your laptop tells you nothing about that.
  • Every photo you send costs your user money.
  • The map needs both internet and Google.
  • More people now just ask an AI "where should I eat in Kigali" instead of opening a site like yours.
Can it fit?

Not yet. But this is the closest one to being fixed.

One job fixes it: make it work without internet. That single job solves the weak signal, the cost of data, and most of the AI problem, all at the same time.

E

Environmental

Nature, weather, and how life here actually runs.

What we think Rwanda's tourism is built on protecting nature. And normal life here follows things like Umuganda and the rainy seasons. Your site has to know about those to be correct.

How it fits

  • Your rule of never inventing ratings suits a country that sells itself as honest and clean.
  • The parks give you strong content that people are already searching for.
  • Telling visitors rules they don't know — like no plastic bags — is useful, and useful is what makes people keep an app.

How it doesn't fit

  • Your opening hours ignore Umuganda. One Saturday a month, your site will say places are open while the country is closed.
  • You say nothing about the best time of year to come, even though rain changes a lot.
  • Gorilla permits are limited on purpose, so you cannot grow there. You have to grow in the rest of the country — where your information is weakest.
Can it fit?

The idea fits. The details don't yet.

Your thinking is right. The problem is being correct, and that can be fixed. Adding things like Umuganda is also something a foreign company would never bother to do — so it helps you twice.

L

Legal

The law — what you must do.

What we think You are now doing well on people's personal information. You are not doing well on photos. And the law will treat you differently the day you start taking money.

How it fits

  • The data law is genuinely handled. People can see, download and delete their information.
  • Right now you only collect enquiries, not money. That keeps you safe for now.
  • Your admin system already lets you fix or remove a listing when a business asks.

How it doesn't fit

  • Your photos come from other people's websites. Some need you to credit the photographer. Some you probably are not allowed to use at all.
  • The day you take money for someone else's tour, you become a middleman. That brings rules about licences and refunds.
  • Your privacy page gives an email address that nobody reads.
  • Earning money means registering a business and paying tax.
Can it fit?

You are fine today. You stop being fine the day you take money.

Do two things before that day: check where every photo came from, and find out what taking payments requires. Both are cheap now and expensive later.

So — can Tembera fit or not?

Yes. Rwanda is a good place for this, and nothing that doesn't fit is permanent.

Four of the six already fit, or nearly do. The two that don't each need one fix: make it work without internet, and sort out the photos before you take money. Neither means starting again. Neither is expensive right now.

But it fits as one thing, not as anything you want. Tembera works as a careful, trusted list of real places — helping the country's tourism plan, used by tourists, by Rwandans abroad, and later by Rwandans at home once you add Kinyarwanda. It does not work as a big free app paid for by adverts. There will never be enough users for that, and the language problem would stop it. No amount of good code can save that version.

One thing decides everything, and it is the same thing the SWOT found: real, checked information about places. It is what makes a government partnership possible. It is what an AI cannot copy. It is what a foreign company won't bother doing. And it is what visitors actually need. Two different ways of looking at your project gave the same answer — that is the strongest sign you can get.
