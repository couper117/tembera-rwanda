S

Strengths

What is good about your project now.

  • Your login is now properly defendedImproved Someone trying passwords gets blocked after 5 attempts — I tested this in a real browser and it works. Stolen login cookies now expire properly, which they didn't before.
  • Your important logic is now testedImproved 78 automatic tests check that search, distances and rankings behave correctly. They run in about one second, so there's no excuse not to run them.
  • You handle personal data better than mostNew A real privacy policy, users can download everything you hold on them, and they can delete their account. Most projects this size have none of this.
  • You used a good, modern setup Next.js, TypeScript and PostgreSQL are what real companies use. This is not a toy project.
  • Everything is stored in the database You can add or change a place from the admin page without touching any code.
  • The admin area is properly locked Every admin page and every admin button checks "are you an admin?" first. Nobody can sneak in by guessing a web address.
  • The site does not lie to users No invented ratings, no fake listings in empty categories. I also fixed the About page, which was still claiming the site has no user accounts — that stopped being true a while ago.
  • It is a real, full product Search, maps, saved places, reviews, bookings and an admin system.

W

Weaknesses

What still needs work. The big ones are first.

  • None of this work is saved yetDo today Everything I just did is sitting loose in your project folder, not saved into git. One wrong command and a full day of work is gone. This is the easiest and most urgent thing on the whole list.
  • The automatic checking has never actually runNew I set it up and tested each step by hand, but GitHub has never run it, because the work isn't pushed yet. Until then it's a script nobody has tried.
  • The new security code is barely testedNew The 78 tests cover search and distances, not the login system. I checked the password blocking by hand once, but that check can't repeat itself. If someone later broke it by accident, no test would notice.
  • Your site cannot send email at allNew So there's no "forgot my password" — a user who forgets is stuck until you run a command for them. There's also no way to check an email address is real, and no booking confirmation.
  • You cannot force anyone to log outNew If someone steals a login cookie, changing your password doesn't kick them out. They stay logged in for up to 30 days.
  • The password blocking resets when you restart It's held in memory, so restarting the site clears the counters. Fine for one server, weaker if you ever run several.
  • Your place information is still not verifiedBiggest Nothing I did touched this. About 200 listings have unconfirmed phone numbers, hours and locations. This is now your single biggest weakness, because it decides whether the site is actually useful to anyone.
  • Three security warnings remain — by your decision They're inside Next.js itself and need a major upgrade to clear. You chose to wait until after launch, which is reasonable: they're hard to exploit here.
  • There's still no way to actually publish the site The checking stops at "it builds". No hosting setup exists, and going live has never been practised.
  • Booking still doesn't take payment The form is now properly validated, but there's no payment, no availability check and no confirmation email.
  • Admins still can't upload a photo Photos have to be web links from six approved sites.
  • Every page still loads all 495 places Fine today. Only a problem if you reach thousands of places.
  • Your project folder is still in OneDrive It keeps locking files and breaking commands. Also — I found three copies of your site server running at once, which was corrupting your builds and making it look like the code was broken. I stopped them.

T

Threats

Dangers from outside. You can't stop these, but you can prepare.

  • Your old Google key is still liveUrgent I removed it from your files, but it's still readable in your project's history. Deleting it is not the same as cancelling it. Until you cancel it in Google, anyone with your code has a working key that bills to your account. This is the only item here that costs real money right now, and only you can do it.
  • Google Maps is free and already on every phone You win by having better Rwandan information than Google has — not by being another map.
  • You depend on Google for your map If they raise prices or change the rules, you follow. Your map also uses an old feature they'll eventually switch off.
  • The privacy policy points to a mailbox nobody readsReduced The law is now mostly handled — people can see and delete their data. But the contact address privacy@tembera.rw is a placeholder. A privacy policy nobody can reply to fails the first time someone complains.
  • Wrong information can cause real harm If someone drives across the country on a wrong address from your site, that's your reputation.
  • Information goes stale Businesses close and numbers change. Some of your photo links are already dead — that's the warning sign.
  • Everything still depends on you You're the only person who knows how this works — and right now, the only copy of a day's work is on your machine.
  • Growing is what will break it The "loads all 495 places" problem only hurts once you're successful.

What to do next

In this order. The first one takes ten minutes and protects everything else.

  • Today — 10 minutes

    Save your work, then cancel the old Google key Commit and push everything to git, so a day's work isn't sitting loose on one machine. Then cancel the old Maps key in Google, and put a real email address in the privacy policy.

  • This week

    Add tests for the login system The password blocking works — I watched it work. But nothing checks it automatically, so a future change could quietly break it. Also, move your project out of OneDrive.

  • This month

    Set up email sending One decision unlocks three things: "forgot my password", checking email addresses are real, and booking confirmations.

  • Next few months

    Get your place data verified Talk to the Rwanda Development Board or district offices about their official lists. This is the one that decides whether people actually rely on your site — and your security work has now made that conversation much easier to have.

  • Watch only — don't act yet

    Speed, and the Next.js upgrade The "loads all places" issue matters at around 5,000 places, not 495. The Next.js 16 upgrade can wait until after launch. Acting on either now would be solving a problem you don't have.
