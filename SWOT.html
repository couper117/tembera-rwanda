S

Strengths

What is good about your project now.

W

Weaknesses

What is left. You chose to leave these for later — that's a reasonable call.

  • Your place information is still not verifiedBiggest Nothing in either round of fixes touched this. About 200 listings have unconfirmed phone numbers, opening hours and locations. This is now clearly your biggest weakness — it decides whether the site is genuinely useful to anyone.
  • Your site cannot send email So there's no "forgot my password" — a user who forgets is stuck until you run a command for them. Also no way to confirm an email is real, and no booking confirmation. One decision fixes all three.
  • There's still no way to actually publish the site The checking stops at "it builds". No hosting is set up, and going live has never been practised.
  • Booking still doesn't take payment Properly validated now, but no payment, no availability check, no confirmation.
  • Admins still can't upload a photo Photos have to be web links from six approved sites.
  • Three security warnings remain — by your decisionDeferred They're inside Next.js itself and need a major upgrade. You chose to wait until after launch, which is sensible: they're hard to exploit here.
  • The password blocking resets when you restart It's held in memory. Fine for one server, weaker if you ever run several.
  • Every page still loads all 495 places Fine today. Only a problem if you reach thousands.
  • Your project folder is still in OneDrive It keeps locking files and breaking commands.

T

Threats

Dangers from outside. You can't stop these, but you can prepare.

  • Your old Google key is still liveUrgent The only urgent item left, and the only one costing real money. Removing it from your files did not cancel it — it's still readable in your project's history. Cancel it in the Google Cloud console.
  • Google Maps is free and already on every phone You win by having better Rwandan information than Google has — not by being another map.
  • You depend on Google for your map If they raise prices or change the rules, you follow. Your map also uses an old feature they'll eventually switch off.
  • The privacy policy points to a mailbox nobody readsReduced The law is mostly handled now — people can see and delete their data. But privacy@tembera.rw is a placeholder. A privacy policy nobody can reply to fails the first time someone complains.
  • Wrong information can cause real harm If someone drives across the country on a wrong address from your site, that's your reputation.
  • Information goes stale Businesses close and numbers change. Some of your photo links are already dead — that's the warning sign.
  • Everything still depends on you You're the only person who knows how this works. Less severe now that the work is backed up on GitHub, but still true.
  • Growing is what will break it The "loads all 495 places" problem only hurts once you're successful.

Part two

PESTEL: the world your project sits in

The SWOT above is mostly about your project. PESTEL is about everything around it — the six outside forces that will shape Tembera whether you think about them or not. Nothing here is something you control. All of it is something you can plan for.

Unlike the SWOT, these aren't sorted into good and bad — a force is just a force. So each point is marked instead: helps you, risk, or watch.

P

Political

Government, policy and stability — who else wants this to succeed.

  • The government actively wants more tourismHelps Tourism is a stated national priority, and Rwanda spends real money promoting itself abroad — the "Visit Rwanda" sponsorships on European football shirts are the visible part. You are pushing in the same direction as national policy, which makes doors easier to open.
  • Rwanda's digital agenda works in your favourHelps Government policy openly favours digital services and local tech. A Rwandan-built digital product is the kind of thing institutions like to be seen supporting.
  • Open visa policy brings more visitorsHelps Rwanda has made entry easier than most countries in the region. More arrivals means more people needing exactly what your site does.
  • Regional instability can cut bookings overnightRisk Unrest in the wider region — particularly near the north-western border — can trigger foreign travel warnings. Those warnings hit gorilla-trekking demand hard, and you have no influence over them. Don't build a business plan that only works in a good year.
  • The government is also a gatekeeperWatch The same body you'd want as a data partner also regulates tourism. That's an opportunity, but it means staying on good terms matters more than it would in a purely private market.
E

Economic

Money — where it comes from, and how people actually pay.

  • Rwanda chose high-value tourism, not high-volumeWatch A gorilla permit costs around $1,500 on purpose — fewer visitors, each spending more. Your booking page already reflects this. It means your money is in a small number of well-off international visitors, not in mass traffic.
  • Conference travel is a real, overlooked segmentHelps Kigali works hard to host international conferences. Those visitors have free evenings, no plans, and money — and they need exactly what a good directory gives them: where to eat, what's nearby, what's worth two hours. Almost nobody is serving them well.
  • Local users can't easily be chargedRisk Charging Rwandan users a subscription won't work at scale. Your realistic money comes from businesses paying to be listed, or commission from international bookings — not from local consumers.
  • Your costs are in dollarsRisk Hosting and Google Maps bill in USD while much of your potential local revenue is in francs. Currency movement squeezes you from both sides, and the Maps bill grows with your success.
  • Mobile money is how people pay hereRisk If you add payments and only support cards, you've excluded most Rwandans. Mobile money must be in the plan from the start, not bolted on later.
S

Social

People — who uses this, in what language, and what they expect.

  • Your project name matches a national campaignWatch "Tembera" means to travel or visit, and the government has run domestic tourism campaigns under very similar wording. That's helpful — the name instantly makes sense to Rwandans. But before you print anything or register a company, check the name doesn't clash with official campaign branding or an existing trademark. Better to find out now than after launch.
  • English alone reaches tourists, not RwandansRisk Your site is English-only. That serves visitors and educated urban users. Kinyarwanda is what unlocks the domestic audience — and domestic tourism is something the government is actively pushing.
  • The memorial category demands careRisk Genocide memorial sites are in your catalogue. These cannot be presented like restaurants — no star ratings, no "book now", no cheerful marketing language. Getting the tone wrong here would cause real offence and real damage. Worth a deliberate design decision, not a default template.
  • Review culture has to be grown, not assumedWatch Your review feature works, but people won't necessarily use it just because it's there. Expect to have to encourage the first wave of reviews rather than waiting for them.
  • The diaspora is a ready-made audienceHelps Rwandans abroad visit home, travel while they're here, and are comfortable with apps. They're easier to reach online than domestic users and they spend like visitors.
T

Technological

Devices, connections and platforms — the conditions your code runs in.

  • Assume a cheap Android phone, not a laptopRisk Most users will be on modest Android devices. Heavy pages feel slow on those in a way they never do on your development machine. Test on a real low-end phone before launch — it is the single most revealing test you can run.
  • Mobile data is expensive, so page weight is a real costRisk Every megabyte you send costs your user money. This is the strongest argument for the offline version mentioned in the SWOT — and a reason to keep images lean.
  • Coverage is worst exactly where tourists goRisk Connectivity is good in Kigali and patchier around the national parks. A travel app that needs a connection fails at the moment of greatest need — standing at a trailhead deciding where to eat.
  • You're tied to Google's map, and its pricingRisk Already in the SWOT, but it's a platform force too: the terms and the bill are set by someone else. Worth knowing that open alternatives exist if the cost ever bites.
  • AI travel assistants are coming for this spaceWatch People increasingly ask an AI "where should I eat in Kigali" instead of opening a directory. Your defence is having verified local data that a general model doesn't — which is the same conclusion the SWOT reached from a different direction.
E

Environmental

Conservation, climate and local customs — Rwanda's tourism is built on these.

  • Conservation is the product, not a side noteHelps Gorillas, national parks and Rwanda's clean-and-green reputation are the main reasons people come. A directory that respects and explains that fits the country's story — and gives you something meaningful to say beyond listings.
  • Umuganda closes things on the last SaturdayRisk On community service morning, many businesses shut and movement is restricted. Your site shows opening hours — if it tells a visitor a place is open when the whole country is at Umuganda, it's wrong in a way that's very visible. This is a concrete thing to handle in your data.
  • Rainy seasons change what's possibleWatch Trekking conditions and some road access shift with the rains. Bookings will be seasonal, and "best time to visit" is genuinely useful content you don't currently offer.
  • Visitor rules are stricter than people expectHelps Rwanda enforces environmental rules — the plastic bag ban is the famous one — and visitors are regularly caught out. Telling them plainly is exactly the kind of practical, trustworthy content that makes a directory worth keeping.
  • Growth at the gorilla sites is capped by policyWatch Permit numbers are limited deliberately to protect the animals. That segment cannot grow much, however good your site gets — so plan growth around the rest of the country.
L

Legal

Rules you must follow — some already handled, some not yet looked at.

  • Data protection: mostly handledHelps Law N° 058/2021 rights are implemented — people can see, download and delete their data. What's left is administrative: a real contact address, and checking whether you need to register formally as a data handler.
  • Your place photos may not be licensedRisk This one is new, and it isn't in the SWOT. Your listings pull images from other people's websites. Some sources are fine, some require crediting the photographer, and some are almost certainly being used without permission. That's a copyright exposure that grows the moment the site is public and commercial. Worth an audit before launch.
  • Taking bookings makes you a travel middlemanRisk Right now you collect enquiries, which is low risk. The moment you take money for someone else's tour, you're an intermediary — which brings licensing, consumer protection and refund obligations. Check the requirements before switching payments on, not after.
  • Listing real businesses carries obligationsWatch Publishing a company's name, contact details and a rating means being able to correct or remove it when they ask. A simple "report a problem with this listing" route protects you and improves your data at the same time.
  • Trading properly means registering properlyWatch Once money changes hands, business registration and tax obligations follow. Not urgent today; unavoidable the moment you monetise.

What PESTEL adds to your plan

Five things the SWOT didn't surface, because they come from outside the code.

  • Before launch

    Audit your place photos Check where each listing image comes from and whether you're allowed to use it. Credit what needs crediting, drop what you can't justify. Copyright complaints are cheap to avoid now and expensive later.

  • Before you print or register anything

    Check the "Tembera" name Confirm it doesn't clash with official campaign branding or a registered trademark. Five minutes of checking against a rename after launch.

  • Soon — a design decision, not a code one

    Decide how memorial sites are presented They shouldn't carry star ratings or booking prompts like a restaurant. Choose deliberately how those listings look and read.

  • Soon — one afternoon

    Test on a cheap Android phone Not your laptop, not an emulator. It's the fastest way to find out what your real users actually experience.

  • When you add payments

    Plan for mobile money and for Umuganda Card-only payment excludes most Rwandans. And opening hours that ignore Umuganda mornings will be visibly wrong once a month.
