Does Tembera fit the SDGs and Vision 2050?

An honest check, done the same way as the PESTEL: how it fits, how it doesn’t, and a straight answer for each one. 19 August 2026.

Read this before anything else

Almost everything below depends on two things: launching, and having verified data. A guide nobody uses helps nobody, however good its intentions. Right now Tembera is not launched, and its listings are researched rather than officially checked.

So most answers here are “yes, once it launches” rather than “yes, today”. That difference matters, and it is kept visible all the way through.

Part one

The UN Sustainable Development Goals

There are 17 goals. Tembera touches a handful of them properly. The rest it does not touch at all, and this page says so — a project that claims all seventeen is describing its marketing, not its work.

8

Decent Work & Economic Growth

Target 8.9 — promote sustainable tourism that creates jobs and supports local culture

How it fits

  • This is the tourism goal, almost word for word. Helping small Rwandan businesses be found is the thing the target asks for.
  • A restaurant or guesthouse that gets found gets customers. That is income, and income is jobs.
  • Covering all 30 districts means the benefit is not only Kigali’s.
  • Booking enquiries go straight to local operators rather than to a foreign agency.

How it doesn’t fit

  • Nobody is using it yet, so no business has gained a single customer from it.
  • Unverified listings can send people to the wrong place, which helps nobody.
  • You cannot yet measure any jobs or income created, so the claim stays a claim.
Does it fit?

Yes — the strongest and most direct of them all.

But it becomes real on launch day, not today. To prove it later, count something simple from the start: how many businesses get found through Tembera, and how often.

9

Industry, Innovation & Infrastructure

Target 9.c — increase access to information and communications technology

How it fits

  • This is digital infrastructure for tourism, built in Rwanda instead of bought from abroad.
  • The skills and the code stay in the country.
  • Built for cheap phones and small data budgets, not expensive devices.

How it doesn’t fit

  • It still needs an internet connection, so it fails exactly where connectivity is weakest.
  • It does not extend access to technology — it uses what already exists.
  • English-only limits who can actually reach it.
Does it fit?

Partly today. Properly once it works offline.

Offline support is the change that turns this from a weak claim into a real one. A guide that works without signal genuinely extends access.

11

Sustainable Cities & Communities

Target 11.4 — protect and safeguard cultural and natural heritage

How it fits

  • Memorial and heritage sites are documented with care, and are never rated, reviewed, priced or promoted.
  • That rule is enforced in the data itself, so a future design change cannot quietly undo it.
  • The commemoration period in April is recognised by the system.
  • Parks and natural sites are presented as places to respect, not products to sell.

How it doesn’t fit

  • Listing a heritage site is not the same as protecting it. The protecting is done by other people.
  • More visitors can mean more wear on a site. A guide that works adds pressure as well as income.
  • There is no conservation information on the site yet — nothing explaining why these places matter.
Does it fit?

Yes — and this is where Tembera is furthest ahead of an ordinary directory.

Most platforms would happily put five stars on a memorial. Refusing to is a real contribution to safeguarding heritage, and it is already built.

17

Partnerships for the Goals

Target 17.17 — encourage effective public-private partnerships

How it fits

  • The proposal to RDB is exactly this shape: public data, private platform, no public money.
  • Corrections flow back, so the official register improves as the guide is used.
  • No exclusivity is asked for, so nobody gets locked in.

How it doesn’t fit

  • There is no partnership yet. There is a meeting.
  • Until RDB agrees, this goal is a plan rather than a contribution.
Does it fit?

Not yet. It fits the moment RDB says yes.

Honest framing for the meeting: do not claim this goal in the room. Offer it as what the partnership would create.

10

Reduced Inequalities

Spreading opportunity beyond the capital

How it fits

  • All 30 districts are treated equally in the system, not as a footnote to Kigali.
  • Small businesses with no website get the same visibility as large ones.
  • Global platforms have little commercial reason to cover rural Rwanda properly. You do.

How it doesn’t fit

  • Your data is thinnest in exactly the rural districts where the need is greatest.
  • English-only currently favours educated urban users — the opposite of the goal.
  • A phone and mobile data are still required, which is its own inequality.
Does it fit?

Partly. The intention is right; the data is not there yet.

Kinyarwanda and better rural coverage are what would make this genuine. Both are on the plan.

12

Responsible Consumption & Production

Target 12.b — monitor the impact of sustainable tourism

How it fits

  • Visitors are told rules they routinely get wrong, such as the plastic bag ban.
  • The app never invents ratings or pads empty categories, so people decide on real information.
  • The planned demand dashboard is, in effect, monitoring tourism patterns.

How it doesn’t fit

  • No environmental information about the places themselves.
  • No way to find businesses that operate sustainably, because that is not recorded.
  • The monitoring part is planned, not built.
Does it fit?

A little. Enough to mention, not enough to lead with.

5

Gender Equality

Visibility for women-owned businesses

How it fits

  • Nothing today. Honestly, nothing.

How it doesn’t fit

  • The system does not record who owns a business, so it cannot surface women-owned ones.
  • No such filter, badge or collection exists.
Does it fit?

No. Not today, and we should not claim it.

It would be easy to add though: one field on a business, one filter, one collection. A genuine opportunity rather than a genuine achievement — and worth doing in phase three.

The other ten goals

Tembera has no honest connection to these, and this analysis does not claim them. Goal 14 is about oceans, which is difficult for a landlocked country.

1 No Poverty 2 Zero Hunger 3 Good Health 4 Quality Education 6 Clean Water 7 Clean Energy 13 Climate Action 14 Life Below Water 15 Life on Land 16 Peace & Justice

Goal 15, Life on Land, is the closest of these — the parks are listed and conservation is part of the story — but a directory does not protect an ecosystem, so it stays out.

Part two

Rwanda Vision 2050

Vision 2050 is Rwanda’s long-term plan: upper-middle income by 2035, high income by 2050. It is organised around five pillars. Here is where Tembera genuinely sits against each — including the one it barely touches.

Check the wording before you use this

The pillar names below are the ones commonly cited. Verify them against the current official Vision 2050 and NST documents before putting them in front of RDB. Getting a national pillar’s name wrong would undercut everything else you say.

1

Human Development

Quality of life, skills and services for Rwandans

How it fits

  • Built for Rwandans as well as visitors — a guide to your own country.
  • Built in Rwanda, so the skills stay here rather than being bought in.
  • Runs on the phone people already own, not one they have to buy.

How it doesn’t fit

  • English-only right now, which shuts out most Rwandans.
  • It is a convenience, not a service like health or education.
Does it fit?

Partly. Kinyarwanda is what makes this pillar real.

2

Competitiveness & Integration

Making Rwanda easier to do business with, and better connected

How it fits

  • Rwanda markets itself very well abroad. The weak point is what happens after arrival — and that is exactly what this fixes.
  • A visitor who finds more to do stays longer and spends more.
  • Conference visitors are a high-value group nobody currently serves well.
  • Tourism earns foreign currency, which is what this pillar is aiming at.

How it doesn’t fit

  • None of it happens until the site launches.
  • No way yet to show that anybody stayed longer or spent more.
Does it fit?

Yes — this is the pillar Tembera fits best.

This is the argument to lead with at RDB. It is their mandate, in their language.

3

Agriculture for Wealth Creation

Farming as a driver of income and exports

How it fits

  • Markets, coffee and tea experiences and farm visits are the kind of thing a guide can send people to.
  • Umuganura, the harvest day, is already in the app’s calendar.

How it doesn’t fit

  • Tembera is a tourism directory. It does not touch farming, yields, exports or rural incomes in any direct way.
  • There is no agri-tourism category today.
Does it fit?

Barely — and we should say so.

Claiming a strong link here would be a stretch, and a stretch is what people remember. If you want a real connection later, an agri-tourism category — coffee tours, tea estates, farm stays, markets — would be an honest one.

4

Urbanisation & Agglomeration

Growing secondary cities, not only Kigali

How it fits

  • Every district is treated equally in the system, not as an afterthought to the capital.
  • Visibility for secondary cities is what brings visitors and spending to them.
  • The planned demand dashboard would show RDB which districts people search for — useful for planning where growth is coming.

How it doesn’t fit

  • Coverage outside Kigali is real but thin, so equal treatment in the system is not yet equal treatment in practice.
  • A directory does not build a city. It points at one.
Does it fit?

Yes, in intention — and verified data is what makes it true.

5

Accountable & Capable State Institutions

Institutions that work well and can be trusted

How it fits

  • Built to carry official data accurately, with a public route for anyone to report an error.
  • Rwandan data protection law is implemented in full, not partly.
  • Every correction is recorded, so there is a trail rather than a rumour.
  • The system never invents information, which is the minimum bar for carrying anything official.

How it doesn’t fit

  • It is a private platform, not an institution.
  • No relationship with any institution exists yet.
Does it fit?

Yes — as a tool an institution could safely use.

This is why the security and privacy work matters commercially and not only technically. It is what makes the platform safe for RDB to be associated with.

So — does it fit?

Yes to both, clearly — but almost all of it is potential, not yet achievement.

Tembera fits four SDGs properly and three Vision 2050 pillars strongly. Nothing here is a stretch, and nothing has been claimed that is not real.
