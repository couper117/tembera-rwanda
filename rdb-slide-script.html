Tembera — RDB slide script

Every word from the presentation, laid out so you can copy it straight into Canva. Nothing here is styled for a slide — it is the text and the structure only.

How to use this

  1. In Canva, start a Presentation (16:9).
  2. Work down this page one slide at a time. Each block is one Canva slide.
  3. Press Copy on a slide to put its text on your clipboard, then paste into Canva and lay it out.
  4. The Layout line tells you the shape — one column, two columns, four boxes, and so on.
  5. Speaker notes go in the notes panel at the bottom of the Canva editor, not on the slide.

Keep the Working now and Planned labels visible on the slides that have them. They are what keep the deck honest — planned features should never look finished.

Design settings for Canva

Green (main)#1F6B4A
Blue (planned)#2A6B8F
Ochre#A8761B
Clay#A63A28
Text#101A16
Background#F2F4F1

Headings: a serif with character. In Canva try Playfair Display, Lora or Source Serif Pro. Regular weight, not bold — large size does the work.

Body text: a plain sans. Inter, Work Sans or Open Sans. Keep body text at 20pt or larger so it reads from the back of the room.

Rule of thumb: slide titles around 40–54pt, body 20–24pt, the small label above each title around 12–14pt in capitals with wide letter spacing.

SLIDE 7 Layout: title, three boxes across
Small labelHow it is built
TitleBuilt to standards a public institution can defend choosing.
Box 1 — Data protection

Law N° 058/2021 implemented: users can see, download and permanently delete everything held about them. A published privacy policy and a reachable contact.

Box 2 — Security

Passwords hashed. Sign-in attempts rate limited. Sessions expire and can be revoked. No default passwords anywhere in the system.

Box 3 — Honesty by design

The system never invents data. No fake ratings, no padded categories, and approximate locations are marked as approximate.

SLIDE 8 Layout: title, subtitle, two columns of bullets. Keep this one calm and plain.
Small labelA deliberate decision
TitleMemorial sites are never rated, reviewed, priced or promoted.
SubtitleGenocide memorials are in the guide, because visitors need to find them. They are not presented like a restaurant.
  • No star ratings. No reviews. No prices.
  • Never placed in "top rated" or promotional rows.
  • A restrained page design, visibly different from a listing.
  • Visiting guidance instead — dress, quiet, photography.
  • The commemoration period in April is recognised by the system.
  • Enforced in the data itself, so it cannot be undone by a design change.
Speaker noteWorth pausing on. The catalogue originally carried a five-star rating on a memorial site, inherited from its source data. It was found and removed deliberately. Being straightforward about that is stronger than implying it was never there. Do not decorate this slide.

SLIDE 12 Layout: title, two columns — left is a list, right is two paragraphs
Small labelUN SDGs — partial, and not claimed
TitleWhere we contribute partly, and where we do not.
Left — Partial contribution
  • Goal 10 — Reduced Inequalities. Coverage of all 30 districts pushes visibility beyond Kigali, where listings are thinnest.
  • Goal 12 — Responsible Consumption. Practical guidance for visitors, including rules they routinely get wrong.
  • Goal 5 — Gender Equality. Women-owned businesses could be identified and surfaced. Not built yet — we are not claiming it.
Right — Not applicable

The remaining goals — including health, water, energy, and Goal 14 on oceans, for a landlocked country — have no honest connection to a tourism directory.

We are not claiming them. A project that maps itself onto all seventeen goals is telling you something about its marketing, not its impact.

Speaker noteThis slide will do more for your credibility than the previous one. Everyone claims SDG alignment; almost nobody says which goals do not apply.
